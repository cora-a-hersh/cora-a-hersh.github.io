Formation and propagation of interannual variability in global subtropical gyres


Figure from Hersh et al., 2025

In the global subtropical oceans, large-scale currents bring waters from the sea surface in the subtropics down to a depth of a few hundred meters, where they travel across the width of entire basins until they eventually re-emerge in upwelling regions at the equator or in western boundary currents. Recent evidence has suggested that year-to-year changes in the water properties that travel through these pathways can survive for long distances as they move toward the tropics. The strength of the connection between the subtropics and tropics through these "ocean tunnels" could have important implications for the predictability of tropical climate variability.

My Ph.D. thesis work focused on the year-to-year variability of the water properties in these currents: how anomalies of water properties like temperature and salinity form at the sea surface in the subtropics, how long they can survive as they travel toward the tropics, and what impact they can have on the variability of upper equatorial waters.

Argo float data quality


Figure from Wong et al., 2020

The Argo float program is a major international collaboration that deploys floating robots throughout the global oceans to measure water properties like temperature, salinity, and pressure down to a depth of 2000 meters (half the average depth of the ocean). Increasingly, new types of floats are also being deployed that can measure chemical and biological properties, along with others that can dive even deeper. This dataset, built over the past two decades, is a revolutionary resource in oceanography and climate studies, with at least several thousand papers published using Argo data to date. With such widespread usage, a clear understanding of the accuracy of Argo sensor data is imperative, especially for long-term climate studies.
